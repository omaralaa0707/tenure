# Tenure — Design Context

## Design Context (rewritten 2026-07-26, sixth pass)

### What changed and why
Five prior rounds patched one complaint at a time without questioning the founding concept: too bland → over-decorated → reverted → "looks like a weird document with a demo chat box" → restructured → broadened → destigmatized → re-storied. Omar's next round of feedback was total: delete it, start over, both the visual design and the underlying concept were wrong.

His brief for the rebuild, in his own words: *"i want a more direct version of the ai employment firm, so basically an ai agency but also not product first. i also want to sell the outcomes not the ai itself, the ai is just the delivery mechanism."*

Read as: a full-service, done-for-you delivery model (not self-serve software), organized entirely around the outcome delivered rather than the AI mechanism, executed plainly rather than through metaphor. **"Basically an agency" describes the delivery model, not the word choice** — the locked brand decision to never say "agency" (valuation/reference-class reasoning from the original brand session) stays; the site just needs to *feel* full-service.

### Users
Founders and owners of service businesses (recruiting, consulting, accounting, law, etc.) losing inquiries because nobody answers fast enough. They land here from a cold email or a referral, want to know in under a minute whether this actually solves their problem, and want to talk to a person, not configure software.

### Brand personality
Three words: **direct, accountable, outcome-first.**

Not clever, not institutional, not a product demo. State the problem, state the fix, prove it works, say what it costs. The previous "employment paperwork" conceit (role scorecards, offer letters, certificate borders, a serif chosen to evoke legal printing) is retired entirely, not reskinned — it was reasoned through carefully at the time, and it was still wrong twice in a row.

### Aesthetic direction
**One typeface, one accent, no metaphor.** Schibsted Grotesk (a Scandinavian news publisher's typeface: built to state a result plainly) carries every weight of the page. No second family, no serif-as-identity. OKLCH neutrals tinted toward a deep pine hue (152°) — the color of a ledger in the black, not a wax seal. Reference class is a modern productized-service or staffing-firm site, not a SaaS product (no configurator, no dashboard chrome) and not a legal document (no double-rule borders, no field/value forms styled as paperwork, no "offer letter" language anywhere in the UI).

**Structural rule that matters most:** the primary call to action is a conversation ("Talk to Omar"), not the live demo. The demo is real, working proof, and it stays on the page, but it's evidence in a "See it work" section, not the hero's centerpiece. This is what makes the page read as a service business asking for a conversation rather than a product asking to be tried.

### Design principles
1. **Outcome first, AI second.** The hero headline never says "AI." The mechanism is named once, plainly, in supporting copy.
2. **Conversation over configuration.** Every primary CTA leads to a human, not a self-serve flow.
3. **One concrete scene beats a stat strip.** A single direct sentence describing the actual problem (a lead going cold over a weekend) does more work than abstract numbers.
4. **No visual metaphor for the business model.** The site should not look like paperwork, a certificate, or a SaaS dashboard. It should look like a confident, plainly-written case for hiring someone.
5. **The demo is proof, not the pitch.** It survives every round because it's real, working, differentiated evidence — but it earns its place as a supporting section, not the reason to load the page.
